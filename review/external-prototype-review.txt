Review: Bellman-Ford arbitrage prototype

Verdict: you're right that it's bad, but not The core math is correct, and every profitit reports is recomputed from current quotes, so it never reports a fake profit. What's broken is everything around
that: with the shipped config it can't find t after running for a while, and everyduration it logs is wrong. Its incremental Bellman-Ford also breaks the textbook algorithm's rules in four places, and it solves a speed problem that doesn't exist.

How I checked it (no project code was changed):
- Existing checks: the 4 tests in review/check.rs all fail against the real source.
- Synthetic market: I replayed the exact mairket with injected mispricings. Ground truthwas every 3- and 4-leg cycle, priced directly from raw bid/ask. 200k updates, 3 random seeds.
- Speed: I timed a from-scratch search on the real code at several graph sizes.

It can't produce its intended result

1. The shipped config.toml makes arbitrage iDT" switches discovery to legacy mode(binance.rs:218). Every coin is then paired only with USDT, and that graph contains no 3-leg cycle at all. The detector can't find anything; you only get triangles by deleting that line.
2. It stops recording new opportunities. Once an opportunity ends, its slot in the tracker is never freed (opportunity_store.rs:146-169). After about 128 different cycles, new ones are dropped with no error. In the simulation, 30% of real detections were already lost this way. A 24-hour run will go silent.
3. Every duration is inflated by the 1 s grars:237). The code computes now - start, where now is already past last_seen + grace. A 3 ms opportunity is logged as about 1000 ms, which ruins the timing numbers the PRD asks for. It should be last_seen - start.
4. The log output can't be read. It prints o no currency names, legs, prices or sizes.The "cycles/s" figure also counts false alarms.

Measured result: the shipped loop catches 96s, with a 3–4 update delay and 81% recall per tick. A fresh Bellman-Ford run after every update catches 100% with no delay. The bugs cost you missed opportunities and noise; the profit recheck stops them from producing fake profits.

Complexity that buys nothing

- The cold-path Bellman-Ford never reaches tndidates every 10 s and main.rs:113 neverreads them. That thread, CandidateSet (which uses the RwLock the PRD forbids), the unused simd-json dependency, and the never-called reset_relax_counts/decay_distances are all dead code.
- The per-tick budget and incremental designt isn't under pressure. Measured on the realcode:

- The exhaustive scan is exact and returns every profitable cycle. Bellman-Ford returns one arbitrary cycle; Sedgewick
  himself says (p. 681) that no efficient alhe PRD bans enumeration, which rules out the exact method even though it is well inside the latency budget.

Market model
- Staleness is modelled wrong. Binance only sends a bookTicker when the quote changes, so a quiet pair can be current after 5 s while a busy pair's 5 s-old quote is badly out of date. What actually matters, whether all legs were quoted at the same moment, isn't measured.
- The 150 µs latency target is irrelevant. Network delay to Binance is milliseconds to hundreds of milliseconds per leg, so co-located traders take these first. The loop also busy-spins a full core; under the fleet's 0.1 CPU limit
  it would stall about 90 ms of every 100 ms

What's correct

- The edge weights -(ln bid + ln(1-f)) and ln ask - ln(1-f) are right: sell at the bid, buy at the ask, fee on every leg.
- Starting every distance at 0 acts as a virtual source reaching every node (Sedgewick Ex. 4.4.43).
- It stores the predecessor edge, not just the node.
- The numerical tolerance is kept separate from the minimum-profit threshold.

The two docs

- Algorithms.pdf is Sedgewick & Wayne, 4th e(book pp. 668–681). The table above is thecomparison.
- infos_astra_6.txt is sound. I checked its worked example: 1.0062 gross, 1.003184 at 10 bps per leg, 0.99717 at 30
  bps. Its :chatgpt-content-reference citatiThe points it stresses (trade size, "onecycle is not the best cycle", exchange vs. receive timestamps, being stuck after a leg fills, rounding) are exactly what this prototype is missing. Its advice to compare Bellman-Ford against bounded enumeration is what my simulation did.

Recommendation

Before fixing any code, find out whether net-of-fee opportunities with real size exist at all. The live recording measures that. If they essentially never do, no fix will help. If they do, the simpler design is:
- replace the hot/cold Bellman-Ford with the
- delete the cold thread and the per-tick budget;
- fix slot reuse and the duration bug;
- log the route, leg prices, quote ages and sizes;
- remove quote_filter from config.toml.

I didn't run cargo test or the benchmarks, because building the full crate needs network access for its dependencies.

The experiment scripts are in the scratchpad, sim\sim.rs and sim\cost.rs.
